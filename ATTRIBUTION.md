# Attribution and Sourcing Policy

This repository contains **original writing about patterns that other people
discovered, named, and published**. The writing is ours. The ideas are theirs.
This file credits every original provider and states plainly how sources are
used.

## The sourcing rule

Every entry in this repository is written from scratch after reading the primary
sources. No source text is copied. No source text is closely paraphrased. Where
a claim comes from a source, that source is cited in the entry with author,
title, edition, and page or section, plus a URL and a verification date where
one exists.

A citation is a pointer, never a transfer of rights. Citing a book here does not
place that book under this repository's licence.

## refactoring.guru

refactoring.guru was used as a **coverage checklist only**, to establish which
patterns, code smells, and refactoring techniques a reader would expect a
reference to contain. No text, illustration, code sample, or structural prose
from that site appears in this repository.

That site's Content Usage Policy permits citing text that is not a substantial
part of an article, and permits up to ten illustrations in total across all
publications, with attribution. This repository uses neither. It uses zero
illustrations and zero text from the site.

Content Usage Policy consulted 2026-08-02 at
https://refactoring.guru/content-usage-policy

Credit where it is due. refactoring.guru, by Alexander Shvets, is an excellent
paid resource. If you want illustrated walkthroughs and a purchasable book, buy
it from the original author at https://refactoring.guru

## Original providers, by family

### Design patterns

Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides. *Design Patterns.
Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software*. Addison-Wesley, 1994. The
twenty three patterns in family 01 originate here.

Christopher Alexander, Sara Ishikawa, and Murray Silverstein. *A Pattern
Language*. Oxford University Press, 1977. The idea of a pattern language, which
the software community borrowed.

### Refactoring and code smells

Martin Fowler, with Kent Beck. *Refactoring. Improving the Design of Existing
Code*. Addison-Wesley. 1st edition 1999, 2nd edition 2018. Families 02 and 03
originate here. Kent Beck contributed the code smells chapter.

### Enterprise application architecture

Martin Fowler. *Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture*.
Addison-Wesley, 2002. Family 06 originates here. Catalogue also published at
https://martinfowler.com/eaaCatalog/

### Enterprise integration

Gregor Hohpe and Bobby Woolf. *Enterprise Integration Patterns*.
Addison-Wesley, 2003. Family 07 originates here. Catalogue also published at
https://www.enterpriseintegrationpatterns.com/patterns/messaging/

### Architecture and concurrency

Frank Buschmann, Regine Meunier, Hans Rohnert, Peter Sommerlad, and Michael
Stal. *Pattern-Oriented Software Architecture, Volume 1. A System of Patterns*.
Wiley, 1996.

Douglas Schmidt, Michael Stal, Hans Rohnert, and Frank Buschmann.
*Pattern-Oriented Software Architecture, Volume 2. Patterns for Concurrent and
Networked Objects*. Wiley, 2000. Family 09 draws heavily on this volume.

Len Bass, Paul Clements, and Rick Kazman. *Software Architecture in Practice*,
4th edition. Addison-Wesley, 2021. Quality attributes and architectural tactics.

### Domain-driven design

Eric Evans. *Domain-Driven Design. Tackling Complexity in the Heart of
Software*. Addison-Wesley, 2003. Family 11 originates here.

Vaughn Vernon. *Implementing Domain-Driven Design*. Addison-Wesley, 2013.

### Microservices

Chris Richardson. *Microservices Patterns*. Manning, 2018, and the pattern
language at https://microservices.io/patterns/ Family 10 follows this
organisation.

Sam Newman. *Building Microservices*, 2nd edition. O'Reilly, 2021.

### Cloud and distributed systems

Microsoft. *Azure Architecture Center, Cloud Design Patterns*.
https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/ Family 08 follows
this catalogue's naming.

Amazon Web Services. *AWS Well-Architected Framework*.
https://aws.amazon.com/architecture/well-architected/

Martin Kleppmann. *Designing Data-Intensive Applications*. O'Reilly, 2017.
Family 12 draws on this for replication, consensus, and storage engines.

### Reliability and operations

Betsy Beyer, Chris Jones, Jennifer Petoff, and Niall Richard Murphy, editors.
*Site Reliability Engineering*. O'Reilly, 2016, and the companion *The Site
Reliability Workbook*, 2018. Free to read at https://sre.google/books/
Families 21 and 22 draw on these.

### Testing

Gerard Meszaros. *xUnit Test Patterns. Refactoring Test Code*. Addison-Wesley,
2007, and the catalogue at http://xunitpatterns.com/ Family 14 originates here.

Martin Fowler. *Mocks Aren't Stubs*, 2007, for the test double taxonomy.
https://martinfowler.com/articles/mocksArentStubs.html

### Security

OWASP Foundation. *Application Security Verification Standard*.
https://owasp.org/www-project-application-security-verification-standard/
Family 15 maps to ASVS requirements where one applies.

### Release and deployment

Jez Humble and David Farley. *Continuous Delivery*. Addison-Wesley, 2010.
Family 20 draws on this.

Martin Fowler. *BlueGreenDeployment*, *CanaryRelease*, *FeatureToggle*, and
*BranchByAbstraction*. https://martinfowler.com/bliki/

### Effective language practice

Joshua Bloch. *Effective Java*, 3rd edition. Addison-Wesley, 2018. Cited where
a language idiom diverges from the GoF form, for example the enum singleton and
the fluent builder.

### AI and agentic patterns

Anthropic. *Building Effective Agents*, 2024.
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents

Model Context Protocol specification.
https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/

Shunyu Yao and others. *ReAct. Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language
Models*. arXiv 2210.03629.

Individual papers are cited per entry in family 17 with their arXiv identifiers.

### Anti-patterns

William Brown, Raphael Malveau, Hays McCormick, and Thomas Mowbray.
*AntiPatterns. Refactoring Software, Architectures, and Projects in Crisis*.
Wiley, 1998. Family 18 originates here.
